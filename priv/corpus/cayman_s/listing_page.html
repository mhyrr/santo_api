This Porsche 987 Cayman got away, but there are more like it here.

42K-Mile 2007 Porsche Cayman S 6-Speed

USA
Sold for USD $33,000 08/30/2017116Comments
View Result
MakePorsche
ModelPorsche 987 Cayman
Era2000s
OriginGerman
LocationLocated in United States

This 2007 Porsche Cayman S is finished in Slate Grey Metallic over Terracotta Full Leather and shows 41,660 miles. It was acquired by the seller in 2015 as a one-owner car, and it has been maintained in stock specification other than Sirius satellite radio and a phone cradle/charger. Power comes from a 3.4-liter flat-six paired to a 6-speed manual transmission. In addition to the optional paint color and interior treatment, equipment includes the Sport Chrono Package without PCM, a Sport Shifter, the Preferred Package, Bi-Xenon Headlamp Package, heated seats, 19″ Sport Design wheels, automatic climate control, the Bose High End Sound Package, painted rear center console, deviated carpeting, and a leather Sport steering wheel. This Porsche carried an original MSRP of $75,180 and is now being offered with its window sticker, a Porsche CoA, service receipts from new, an accident-free Carfax, and a clean Pennsylvania title in the seller’s name.

This Cayman retains all of its factory paint and body panels to the seller’s knowledge. A clear bra was recently removed from the front bumper and hood. The seller reports the exterior is clean aside from an area with small paint chips on the upper driver’s door highlighted in the photo gallery below. The seller reportedly garages the car and does not drive it in inclement winter.

This Cayman is fitted with optional 19″ Sport Design wheels that feature colored Porsche crest center caps. They are shod in Michelin Pilot Super Sport tires all around.

Inside, the cabin is described as clean throughout, with wear showing on the driver’s seat cushion and outer bolster as shown above.

All features function properly per the seller, and the odometer currently shows 41,660 miles. The aftermarket phone cradle/charger can be seen above.

The 3.4-liter flat-six developed peaks of 295 horsepower at 6,250 rpm and 252 lb-ft of torque at 4,400 rpm when new. According to the seller, the 40k-mile service was completed in July 2017 and included an oil change and new cabin filters. The receipt for this work comes with the car, as does all other service documentation from new.

Additional undercarriage pictures are displayed in the photo gallery.

The accident-free Carfax report was run on 8/17/2017. It displays two owners, seven service history records, and registration in Florida and Pennsylvania.

The Certificate of Authenticity is displayed above, and the original window sticker is shown in the photo gallery below.

“Enjoy your new Porsche, sir! Just don’t drive it or let time pass, or it will be worth much, much less.”

But, I’m an amateur driving enthusiast, not a car collector. I see a ten-year-old Cayman S 6-Speed with 42K miles, and I wince at the fun not had, the time wasted by the previous owner while this sat in a garage. I see a clean rev report on a Porsche with sport chrono and a stick and think either the owner(s) were pro drivers who never made mistakes, or just completely afraid of the accelerator pedal.

Then there’s the economic case to driving and enjoying these cars:

This car was around $75K new – 42K miles = $33K now, yes?
$42K in depreciation for 42K miles, so one dollar of depreciation per mile.

The ’06 Atlas was $74K new – 88K miles = $20K now.
$55K in depreciation for 88K miles, that’s 63 cents of depreciation for each mile.

So, the Atlas ’06 was $13K (39%) less for the new owner, and the previous owner(s) suffered far less depreciation per mile of pure fun while actually driving the car. I call that a win win for both buyer and seller.

Not so much of a win for those who buy and don’t drive low mileage garage queens.

But, again, I’m just a guy who loves to drive fast and used to be decent at math.
I could be wrong. My wife claims that actually does happen, and I trust her.
